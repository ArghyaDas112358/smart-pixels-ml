The Adjacency Trap

How the slice-discovery campaign found and escaped the two failure modes of learned readout search — and then discovered that the loss function had been hiding a third.live campaign

The O21 mechanism arms are complete; the cold zero-prior proof runs (O21.v2a.2 / v2c.2) are queued. Numbers are the banked bests at the time of writing.

TL;DR. The chip reads out only 2 of 101 waveform time-slices plus 3 comparator thresholds. Every gradient router we built chose either the wrong region (late slices) or the wrong shape (two adjacent slices). Three redesigned search mechanisms fixed the shape problem; then replacing the training loss fixed something nobody was looking for.

champion · [10, 20] at [8.9, 21.5, 49.7] mV → σα 8.24°, σβ 5.99°, σx 6.64 µm
previous gold standard: hand-imposed [11, 26] → σα 12.7°, σβ 8.3°

Both angle resolutions are project records, position is intact, and the uncertainties are honestly calibrated for the first time — found by free search with nothing handed to it.

1 · The problem the ASIC sets us

Each pixel cluster is a 16×16 array whose charge waveform is sampled at 101 time points. The readout chip cannot ship waveforms — it ships two slice indices and three thresholds, a 2-bit encoding of two chosen time samples. Everything we train is an offline bootstrapping tool whose only job is to choose those five numbers. The regression target is a 4-D Gaussian over (x, y, cot α, cot β): position and track angles.

The tension: position information is everywhere in the waveform, but angle information lives in how the pulse evolves over time. One sample constrains angles poorly; two constrain them through their difference, so the useful signal grows with the time separation — the "lever arm" — between the chosen slices.

2 · Seven campaigns, three diseases

O11–O13
Constraints alone can't fix it.

Correlation floors were gameable by shrinkage; per-angle multipliers reached early slices but destroyed position (σx up to 56 µm). The router always drifted to the late window (~69–73): good position, collapsed angles.

O15
Search was never the bottleneck.

An annealed smoothing kernel made search essentially perfect — three seeds agreeing to 0.5 mV — and converged on the same late window. The objective's local gradients prefer late slices.

O17
Disease #1: capacity.

Deepening the regression head (1280→256→128→64→14, the model we now call ViT_d) with the router frozen at [11, 26] turned a 1-in-5 lucky basin into a certainty: every escapee reached σα ≈ 13°, σx ≈ 7 µm — the first configuration solving angles and position together. But it had to be told the slices.

O18
Capacity doesn't transfer to search.

The same ViT_d with a free router re-found the late window. Cold, the early basin doesn't exist yet — the head hasn't learned early-slice features, so early slices score badly, so the router never trains them. A chicken-and-egg.

O20
Disease #2: search geometry.

Warm-starting from an O17 checkpoint (router kept fresh — no answer leaked) flipped the router early within ~300 epochs. But it settled on adjacent pairs (12–14). Two causes: walking one slice from 13 toward 26 crosses a valley of worse intermediate pairs, and the smoothing kernel operates along the slice axis, structurally forcing contiguous preferences. That is the adjacency trap.

O21
Three attacks on the geometry — two worked.

All arms share the warm start and recipe; only the search mechanism differs, which makes the failure informative.

O21.v2
Disease #3: the loss itself.

Retraining the same arms under a corrected loss moved every angle number by ~30%. Details in §5.

3 · The three search mechanisms

The router faces a discrete choice — which 2 of 101 — so it can't be gradient-descended directly. The baseline keeps one score θ per slice and samples a pair with probability ∝ exp(θa+θb). Each arm replaces that differently.

a · Pair-lattice — fix the smoothing geometry

Instead of one logit per slice, give the router one free logit per pair (all 5,050) and anneal a 2-D Gaussian over the pair lattice. In that geometry the neighbours of (11, 26) are (12, 25) and (10, 27) — wide pairs are neighbours of wide pairs — so coarse-to-fine annealing explores pair width the way the old kernel explored pair location.

b · Two independent routers — make "one early, one late" native

A separate softmax per readout slot, each with its own annealed kernel, plus a differentiable anti-overlap penalty. Elegant, and it failed: each router still smooths along the slice axis, both get pulled into the same locally-good region, and the anti-overlap term separates them by a slice or two, not by fifteen. It stayed adjacent in both loss versions — which makes it the campaign's control: since a and c escaped and b didn't, the escape is attributable to pair-space geometry and exploration, not to the warm start all three share.

c · UCB bandit — exploration as the algorithm

Abandon router gradients entirely. The 5,050 pairs are bandit arms; reward is the realised batch loss. Upper Confidence Bound scores each arm as estimated value plus a bonus that grows the longer an arm goes untried, so it provably cannot permanently ignore a distant basin. Discounted (γ = 0.9995) because the problem is non-stationary — an arm genuinely improves as the head trains on it. It sweeps every arm once (~163 epochs) before concentrating.

5 · The third disease: the loss was Huberising its own worst cases

The training loss built a probability density, clipped it to [1e−9, 1e9], then took the log — capping every event's NLL at 20.72. On a trained model that looked harmless: 4,992 of 5,000 events matched the exact NLL to 1e−15. But the 8 that didn't (0.16%) were capped while their true NLL was 27–211. Those are precisely the catastrophically mis-fit events — and residual standard deviation is tail-dominated. Capping their loss capped their gradients, so the network never learned to fix them.

The replacement (contributed by the ASIC/symbolic-regression side of the collaboration, ported here tfp-free) removes the clip and swaps the ReLU covariance diagonal for softplus — smooth, strictly positive, no dead zone where a diagonal can pin at its floor. Measured at a cold initialisation, the old loss produces a gradient norm of exactly 0; the new one, 139.

The loss effect is universal, not pair-luck. Arm b is stuck on adjacent slices in both versions — same trap, same geometry — and still gains 6.5° of cot α from the loss change alone. Three further signatures point the same way: calibration moved from over-confident (pulls 1.2–1.5 under the old loss) to honest (0.88–1.04); the best checkpoint moved from epoch 1,318 to 4,567 of 5,000, meaning the clipped objective went flat a quarter of the way through while the corrected one was still improving at the end; and the winning pair got narrower (10 slices, not 13), so the clean loss extracts more angle information from less lever arm.

6 · Is the answer inherited? The zero-prior proof

Every result above was warm-started from an O17 checkpoint trained with the router frozen at [11, 26], so a fair reviewer asks whether the discovered pairs merely echo that prior. The audit is worth stating precisely:

  • The router was never given the answer. All router parameters initialise to zeros — exactly uniform over all 5,050 pairs. The checkpoint's θ literally encodes [11, 26] as +30 pins, and the transfer explicitly excludes the router and asserts θ is untouched before and after.
  • Thresholds were inherited in the warm runs (the quantiser layer is transferred), starting at O17's trained ≈[12.3, 26.8, 63.9] mV rather than a random draw — though they then moved a long way, ending near [8.9, 21.5, 49.7].
  • The representation is a genuine prior. The backbone was optimised to read slices 11 and 26. That was the deliberate premise of O20, but it does mean the discovered pairs carry inheritance.

Two pieces of evidence already argue against contamination: the bandit swept [11, 26] itself and rejected it in favour of [10, 48] and [8, 86]; and the cold controls O15 and O18, with no warm start at all, both went to the late window — so the architecture has no built-in early preference.

The decisive test is now running as O21.v2a.2 and O21.v2c.2: ViT_d + loss v2, both search mechanisms, three seeds each, 10,000 epochs, and nothing inherited — random backbone and head, router from uniform, thresholds from a random draw. If these land on the same slice-and-threshold family, the earlier results are proven free of warm-start bias and the thresholds are proven optimal rather than inherited.

7 · Honest caveats

  • One seed per mechanism in the completed arms. The cold campaign runs three per mechanism.
  • NLLs are not comparable across the loss change — different dialect, roughly 7 nats/event of pure artifact if cross-scored. Only resolutions and pulls compare, which is why NLL is absent from the comparison tables above.
  • Softplus and clip-removal changed together, so the resolution gain isn't cleanly attributed between them. The tail argument explains the σ change and the dead-gradient argument explains the training stability, but a one-seed ablation (v2 loss with the clip restored) would settle it.
  • v2a had not converged at epoch 4,567 of 5,000 — a longer run may still be leaving performance on the table.
  • Thresholds are pair-specific. The [8.9, 21.5, 49.7] mV set belongs to [10, 20] and would be re-derived for whichever pair ships.

8 · What's next

  • Finish the cold zero-prior runs — the bias question, settled.
  • A frozen-router confirmation on the winning pair under loss v2, multiple seeds, no discovery machinery: the mechanism-independent number that would actually ship.
  • The clip-vs-softplus ablation, to attribute the gain.
  • Feed the lever-arm result to the ASIC/FPGA side: angle resolution improves steeply with slice separation and then plateaus, so a front-end that can latch two well-separated samples buys angle performance nearly for free.
